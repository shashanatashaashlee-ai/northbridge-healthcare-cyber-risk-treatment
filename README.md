# Northbridge Community Health – Cyber Risk Treatment & Management

## Project Overview

This project presents a Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) case study focused on ransomware risk management within a fictional healthcare organisation, Northbridge Community Health.

The organisation operates three clinics and faces cybersecurity risks that could affect the availability of clinical systems, confidentiality of sensitive patient information, supplier access and incident detection capabilities.

The objective of this project was to assess the identified risks, prioritise exposures, evaluate treatment strategies and develop a cost-conscious risk treatment plan within a first-year budget of **$100,000**.

The project demonstrates how cybersecurity risks can be translated into practical treatment decisions, financial considerations and executive-level reporting.

## Business Scenario

Northbridge Community Health operates three healthcare clinics and relies on technology to support:

- Patient records and sensitive health information.
- Clinic booking and operational systems.
- Supplier integrations and connected accounts.
- Security monitoring and incident response.

A ransomware threat could disrupt clinic operations, compromise sensitive information and affect the organisation's ability to deliver healthcare services.

An additional concern was that the organisation's clinics could sustain operations using paper-based bookings for only four hours.

This highlighted the importance of business continuity, backup integrity and tested recovery procedures.

## Project Objectives

The key objectives were to:

1. Identify and assess the organisation's major ransomware-related risks.
2. Establish inherent risk scores using a likelihood and impact methodology.
3. Prioritise risks according to their severity.
4. Evaluate risk treatment strategies, including mitigation, avoidance, transfer and acceptance.
5. Develop a treatment portfolio within the $100,000 budget.
6. Calculate potential cyber insurance payouts and retained financial exposure.
7. Establish proposed residual risk scores.
8. Develop a 30-day implementation roadmap.
9. Present findings through an executive risk dashboard and governance decision log.

## Risk Assessment

Four primary risks were identified and assessed.

| Risk | Initial Score | Rating |
|---|---:|---|
| Service disruption | 20 | Critical |
| Sensitive-data loss | 20 | Critical |
| Unnecessary supplier exposure | 12 | High |
| Delayed detection | 16 | High |

Risk scores were calculated using:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

The assessment used the following classification:

- Low: 1–4
- Medium: 5–9
- High: 10–16
- Critical: 17–25

## Risk Treatment Strategy

The proposed treatment portfolio prioritises four interventions.

| Treatment | Budget |
|---|---:|
| Retire unused supplier workflow | $5,000 |
| MFA and access review | $35,000 |
| Protected backups and recovery testing | $35,000 |
| Managed detection and escalation | $20,000 |
| **Total** | **$95,000** |

The proposed portfolio remains within the $100,000 first-year budget, leaving $5,000 unallocated.

The treatment decisions focus on reducing unnecessary access, strengthening identity controls, improving recovery capability and enhancing threat detection.

## Important Risk Treatment Consideration

During the case study, a restoration test failed, revealing a weakness in the organisation's recovery capability.

The organisation could only sustain paper-based bookings for four hours.

Consequently, service disruption remains a Critical risk until a successful, witnessed restoration test and integrity check demonstrate that recovery capability is effective.

Proposed residual risk scores are targets, not confirmed outcomes. They must be validated after implementation and testing.

## Cyber Insurance Analysis

The project also evaluates the financial implications of transferring some cyber risk through insurance.

The case study uses:

- Deductible: $25,000
- Policy payout cap: $250,000

Two covered-loss scenarios were evaluated:

| Covered Loss | Insurer Payment | Retained Loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| $180,000 | $155,000 | $25,000 |
| $400,000 | $250,000 | $150,000 |

An excluded claim was also considered, demonstrating that the organisation would retain the full excluded loss.

The annual insurance premium was not provided, so the total cost of insurance could not be calculated.

## Implementation Roadmap

A 30-day treatment implementation plan was developed, assigning:

- Specific actions and deliverables.
- Accountable owners.
- Budget allocations.
- Implementation deadlines.
- Effectiveness testing requirements.

The plan emphasises evidence-based risk reduction rather than simply marking controls as implemented.

## Deliverables

The Excel workbook contains the following components:

1. **Executive Dashboard** – Executive overview of the risk position, budget and proposed treatment decisions.
2. **Risk Intelligence Register** – Risk statements, likelihood, impact, scores, treatment decisions and evidence requirements.
3. **Treatment Investment** – Budget allocation, selected interventions and deferred options.
4. **Insurance & Exposure** – Insurance payout calculations and retained financial exposure.
5. **30-Day Delivery Plan** – Implementation actions, owners, costs and effectiveness tests.
6. **Governance & Decisions** – Board decisions, risk acceptance considerations and outstanding exposures.
7. **Risk Chart** – Visual comparison of initial risk scores and proposed residual risk targets.
